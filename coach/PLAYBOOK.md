# Professional Speaking Playbook

A reference for sounding structured and articulate when speaking, written for fluent non-native English speakers. Use it with the Speech Coach (see `INSTRUCTIONS.md`).

## 1. Six principles

1. **Answer first.** Say the conclusion in your first sentence. Context comes after.
2. **One idea per sentence.** Aim for under 20 words. If you hear "which… that… because…", stop and start a new sentence.
3. **Signpost.** Tell the listener the shape before you fill it: "Two reasons."
4. **Use threes, at most.** Two or three points are memorable; five are a list nobody keeps.
5. **Be concrete.** A number or an example beats an adjective. "Cut onboarding from 9 days to 2" beats "significantly improved onboarding".
6. **Land the close.** End by restating the point or the next step. Don't trail off with "…so, yeah".

## 2. Structures

### Answer-first (PREP)
**When:** any direct question, opinion or recommendation.
**Template:** Point → Reason → Example → Point.
> "I'd launch in the UK first. The licensing is already in place, so we save six months. When we did the same in Spain, approval alone took eight. So: UK first, Spain in Q3."

### What / So what / Now what
**When:** updates, reacting to news, contributing in meetings.
**Template:** What happened → Why it matters → What we should do.
> "Visa announced stablecoin settlement in Europe. That means our partners will ask for it within a year. We should scope a pilot this quarter."

### Problem / Solution / Benefit
**When:** pitching an idea, asking for budget.
> "Merchants abandon checkout when payment fails. A smart retry layer fixes most soft declines. That recovers roughly [x]% of lost revenue."

### SCQA
**When:** briefing a senior audience, opening a presentation.
**Template:** Situation (agreed context) → Complication (what changed) → Question (what we must decide) → Answer.

### STAR + L
**When:** behavioural interview questions ("Tell me about a time…").
**Template:** Situation (1 sentence) → Task (1 sentence) → Action (most of the time, use "I") → Result (a number) → Lesson (1 sentence).
**Trap:** spending half the answer on the situation. Get to the action within 20 seconds.

### Disagree constructively (ACA)
**When:** pushing back on a colleague or senior.
**Template:** Acknowledge → Concern → Alternative.
> "I see why speed matters here. My concern is that we'd ship without fraud checks. Could we launch to 10% of traffic first?"

### Past / Present / Future
**When:** telling your career story, explaining how something evolved, setting a vision.

### "I don't know yet"
**When:** you don't have the answer.
> "I don't know yet. What I'd check first is X. I'll come back to you by Thursday."
Saying this cleanly sounds more senior than improvising.

## 3. Sentence patterns

| Pattern | Template | Instead of |
|---|---|---|
| Headline first | "The short answer is X." | Building up to X over three sentences |
| Number the points | "Two reasons. First… Second…" | "There are several reasons, one is that…" |
| Contrast | "It's not X, it's Y." | "It's more like, in a certain sense, Y" |
| Split the chain | Two short sentences | One sentence with "which… that… because…" |
| Verb, not noun | "We decided" | "We took the decision to" |
| Own it | "I led / I decided" | "It was decided / we did" (in interviews) |
| Quantify | "From 9 days to 2" | "Much faster" |
| Recap | "So, to sum up: X." | Stopping mid-thought |

## 4. Phrase bank

**Opening**
- "The short answer is…"
- "Let me give you the headline first."
- "There are two things to know here."

**Signposting and transitions**
- "First… Second… Finally…"
- "The bigger issue is…"
- "Here's why that matters."
- "Which brings me to…"
- "To give you an example…"

**Buying time (instead of filling silence)**
- "That's a good question. Let me think for a second." *(then pause, silently)*
- "There are a few ways to look at it. The most useful is…"

**Calibrated confidence (instead of vague hedging)**
- "I'm confident that…" / "I'm fairly sure that…"
- "My working assumption is…"
- "The data says X; my read is Y."

**Disagreeing**
- "I see it differently."
- "I'd push back on one point."
- "That works if X. My concern is Y."

**Closing**
- "So my recommendation is…"
- "The next step is…"
- "In short: …"

## 5. Italian → English traps

**Structure**
- **The long Italian sentence.** Italian rewards long, elegant subordinate chains. Spoken English punishes them. Split at every "which", "that" or "because" that starts a third clause.
- **Context before the point.** Italian often builds context first, then the conclusion. In English business settings, the conclusion comes first.
- **"So…" at the start of every sentence** (from *quindi*). Use it once, to conclude.
- **"Let's say…"** (from *diciamo*) as a filler. Drop it.

**False friends**

| Avoid | Means in English | You probably mean |
|---|---|---|
| Actually (for *attualmente*) | In fact | Currently / at the moment |
| Eventually (for *eventualmente*) | In the end | If needed / possibly |
| Argument (for *argomento*) | A disagreement | Topic |
| Sensible (for *sensibile*) | Reasonable | Sensitive |
| Control (for *controllare*) | Direct / restrict | Check |
| Assist (for *assistere*) | Help | Attend / witness |
| Realise (for *realizzare*) | Understand | Build / deliver |
| Pretend (for *pretendere*) | Fake | Expect / demand |
| Fundamental (overused) | Foundational | Key / critical |

**Grammar habits**
- "Since three years" → "for three years".
- "Explain me" → "explain to me"; "say me" → "tell me".
- "Make a question" → "ask a question".
- "Informations / feedbacks / advices" → no plural: information, feedback, advice.
- "In this moment" → "at the moment" / "right now".

## 6. Words to cut

basically · actually (as filler) · a little bit · kind of / sort of · to be honest · "I think that" before a recommendation (just recommend) · very / really (use a stronger word or a number).

## 7. Before and after

**Question:** "Why should we prioritise this project?"

> **Before (82 words):** "So, actually, it's a bit complex, because if you look at the situation of the market, which is changing a lot in this moment, and also considering that our competitors, which are moving quite fast, have already started to work on stablecoin settlement, I think that it's quite fundamental that we start to do something, also because the regulation, let's say, is becoming clearer, and so eventually we could be in a position to lead."

> **After (38 words):** "Because the window is closing. Two reasons. First, competitors are already building stablecoin settlement. Second, regulation is now clear enough to move. If we start this quarter, we can still lead. If we wait, we follow."

What changed: answer first, numbered points, one idea per sentence, false friends removed, a clean close.
